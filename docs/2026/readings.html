Readings

Here is the Fall 2026 reading list. Readings are organized by unit and alphabetized by the first author’s surname.

Books used in this course

Assigned excerpts from the following books will be provided on Canvas:

  • Angrist and Pischke, Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist’s Companion
  • Bellemare, Doing Economics: What You Should Have Learned in Grad School—But Didn’t
  • Champ, Boyle, and Brown, editors, A Primer on Nonmarket Valuation
  • Ethridge, Research Methodology in Applied Economics
  • Greenlaw, Doing Economics: A Guide to Understanding and Carrying Out Economic Research

All other readings, including articles, handouts, course notes, and reviewer guidelines, will also be available on Canvas.

Unit 1: Foundations

  • Ethridge, Don E. “Research and Methodology.” Research Methodology in Applied Economics: Organizing, Planning, and Conducting Economic Research, 2nd ed., Blackwell Publishing, 2004, pp. 15–35. Chapter 2.

  • Ethridge, Don E. “Methodological Concepts and Perspectives.” Research Methodology in Applied Economics: Organizing, Planning, and Conducting Economic Research, 2nd ed., Blackwell Publishing, 2004, pp. 36–57. Chapter 3.

  • Ethridge, Don E. “Philosophical Foundations.” Research Methodology in Applied Economics: Organizing, Planning, and Conducting Economic Research, 2nd ed., Blackwell Publishing, 2004, pp. 58–79. Chapter 4.

  • Greenlaw, Steven A. “What Is Research?” Doing Economics: A Guide to Understanding and Carrying Out Economic Research, Houghton Mifflin, 2006, pp. 1–10. Chapter 1.

  • Kenyon, Ralph E., Jr. “Popper’s Philosophy of Science.” 1984. Unpublished essay.

  • Larue, Louis. “A Defense of Reasonable Pluralism in Economics.” Journal of Economic Methodology, vol. 29, no. 4, 2022, pp. 294–308. https://doi.org/10.1080/1350178X.2022.2112266.

  • Machlup, Fritz. “Why Economists Disagree.” Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society, vol. 109, no. 1, 1965, pp. 1–7. JSTOR, https://www.jstor.org/stable/985773.

  • McCloskey, Donald N. “How Economists Persuade.” Journal of Economic Methodology, vol. 1, no. 1, 1994, pp. 15–32. https://doi.org/10.1080/13501789400000002.

Unit 2: Research Problem

  • Davis, Donald R. “Ph.D. Thesis Research: Where Do I Start?” Columbia University, n.d. Course notes.

  • Dudenhefer, Paul. A Guide to Writing in Economics. EcoTeach Center and Department of Economics, Duke University, Dec. 2009. Sections 5–6.

  • Ethridge, Don E. “Planning the Research.” Research Methodology in Applied Economics: Organizing, Planning, and Conducting Economic Research, 2nd ed., Blackwell Publishing, 2004, pp. 83–99. Chapter 5.

  • Ethridge, Don E. “The Research Problem and Objectives.” Research Methodology in Applied Economics: Organizing, Planning, and Conducting Economic Research, 2nd ed., Blackwell Publishing, 2004, pp. 100–12. Chapter 6.

  • Greenlaw, Steven A. “Overview of the Research Process in Economics.” Doing Economics: A Guide to Understanding and Carrying Out Economic Research, Houghton Mifflin, 2006, pp. 11–30. Chapter 2.

  • Varian, Hal R. “How to Build an Economic Model in Your Spare Time.” The American Economist, vol. 41, no. 2, 1997, pp. 3–10. https://doi.org/10.1177/056943459704100201. Assigned pages 1–3 of the course PDF.

Unit 3: Introduction and Literature Review

  • Head, Keith. “The Introduction Formula.” Keith Head, University of British Columbia, n.d., https://blogs.ubc.ca/khead/research/research-advice/formula. Accessed 14 Aug. 2026.

  • Laubepin, Frederique. How to Read (and Understand) a Social Science Journal Article. Inter-university Consortium for Political and Social Research, 2013.

  • Mankiw, N. Gregory. “How to Write Well.” Greg Mankiw’s Blog, 7 Oct. 2006, https://gregmankiw.blogspot.com/2006/10/how-to-write-well.html. Accessed 14 Aug. 2026.

  • Romer, David. “Rules for Making It Through Graduate School and Finishing Your Dissertation: ‘Out in Five.’” n.d. Handout.

Unit 4: Theory

  • Chouinard, Hayley H. “Theory Notes.” EconS 529, 2015. Course notes.

  • Ethridge, Don E. “The Conceptual Framework.” Research Methodology in Applied Economics: Organizing, Planning, and Conducting Economic Research, 2nd ed., Blackwell Publishing, 2004, pp. 127–39. Chapter 8.

  • Romer, Paul M. “Mathiness in the Theory of Economic Growth.” American Economic Review: Papers & Proceedings, vol. 105, no. 5, 2015, pp. 89–93. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.p20151066.

Unit 5: Data

  • Dominitz, Jeff, and Charles F. Manski. “More Data or Better Data? A Statistical Decision Problem.” The Review of Economic Studies, vol. 84, no. 4, 2017, pp. 1583–1605. https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdx005.

  • Athey, Susan, and Guido W. Imbens. “The State of Applied Econometrics: Causality and Policy Evaluation.” Journal of Economic Perspectives, vol. 31, no. 2, 2017, pp. 3–32. https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.31.2.3.
