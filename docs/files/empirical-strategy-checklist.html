AREC 570 Empirical Strategy Checklist

Unit 6 · Methods of Analysis

Author

Lauren Chenarides

Use this checklist as you are preparing to write your empirical section. This checklist follows the data checklist from Lecture 5.5. Rows marked [6.x] are covered in that lecture. Sources: B = Bellemare, “How to Write Applied Papers in Economics,” Section 5; AJAE = AJAE Author Guidelines and Author’s Final Checklist.

# Relevant section Purpose
1 Estimation strategy Prediction to equation [6.1]. Which testable prediction from your conceptual framework (Unit 4) each equation tests.
2 Estimation strategy Estimating equation [6.1, B]. The equation, with every variable and coefficient defined, subscripts ordered from smallest to largest unit, and a separate coefficient label for each specification.
3 Estimation strategy Unit of observation [6.1]. What one row is. It matches the observation unit and key in your data section (Unit 5).
4 Estimation strategy Parameter of interest [6.1]. Which coefficient answers the question, its units, how to read a one-unit change, and whether it has a causal interpretation (if not, what it describes).
5 Estimation strategy Estimator [6.1, B]. The estimation method (for example, OLS, linear probability model, probit, maximum likelihood, GMM) and why.
6 Estimation strategy Inference [6.1, B]. The null hypothesis; what the standard errors are robust to; the clustering level and why; how few clusters are handled; whether sampling weights are used.
7 Identification strategy Identifying assumption [6.1]. The assumption in one plain sentence, with no notation.
8 Identification strategy Identifying variation [6.1]. Which comparison (across units, over time, at a threshold, from assignment) moves the treatment and not the outcome directly.
9 Identification strategy Threats [6.1, B]. Whether each applies, and how the paper handles it in the design or a robustness check: reverse causality; unobserved heterogeneity; measurement error; spillovers across units or periods.
10 Depends on the design If the paper estimates a structural model [6.2]. The primitives; which variation identifies which parameter; the estimator in one paragraph, details in an appendix; fit or validation; the counterfactuals the model is used for.
11 Depends on the design If the paper uses a prediction step [6.3]. Which step is prediction and which is a causal parameter; how the predictor is tuned and evaluated; how the prediction error affects the parameter of interest.
12 Depends on the design If the paper collects primary data or runs an experiment [6.4, AJAE]. Assignment or sampling; sample size and why it is enough; IRB approval; the instrument or instructions in an appendix that does not identify the authors.
13 Depends on the design If the paper uses qualitative or mixed methods [6.4]. How cases or participants are selected; how evidence is coded; what question the qualitative evidence answers that the quantitative evidence cannot.
14 Scope and placement What the design does not identify [6.5]. Parameters, mechanisms, or populations outside the estimand; limits on external validity.
15 Scope and placement Robustness and sensitivity [6.5, B]. Three checks a referee would request, each tied to a threat in row 9 or to the inference choices in row 6.
16 Scope and placement Appendix [AJAE]. Which implementation details move to the appendix, cited in the text as an online appendix, not “available on request.”
17 Scope and placement Replication [AJAE]. The data, code or pseudo-code, and README to be shared, or the confidentiality restriction to raise with the editor at submission.
