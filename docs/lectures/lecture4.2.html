Chouinard

Theory has a specific job

Chouinard argues that every paper has a theoretical foundation whether or not the author makes it explicit (Chouinard 2015).

For an empirical paper the theory section should

  • Explain the mechanism behind the research question
  • State the assumptions needed for that mechanism
  • Derive the hypotheses evaluated with data
  • Connect theoretical concepts to empirical measures
  • Bound the conclusions the evidence can support

The role of theory depends on the contribution

Theoretical paper Empirical paper
Develop or modify a model Apply theory to explain the empirical question
Establish analytical results Derive hypotheses and comparative predictions
Show what changes relative to prior theory Connect concepts to observed measures
Treat the model as a central contribution Use the model to discipline the empirical argument

Both require explicit assumptions and a coherent sequence of reasoning.

Source: Chouinard (2015)

Match the detail to the theoretical contribution

Apply established theory

Summarize the relevant result and explain how it applies to the research problem

Modify established theory

State the baseline model identify the change and show how the result changes

Develop new theory

Define the setting assumptions relationships and results in sufficient detail to establish the contribution

The amount of theory should reflect its role in the paper.

Chouinard cautions against rederiving familiar theory when the contribution is an application. More detail is warranted when the paper changes the model or contributes new theory.

[Sources] - Chouinard (2015/Sources)

Build from infrastructure to superstructure

Introduce each component before asking the reader to interpret the completed model.

  1. Define the setting and relevant agents
  2. Identify choices and constraints
  3. State the maintained assumptions
  4. Introduce the relationships among model objects
  5. Derive and interpret the result
  6. Connect the result to the empirical question

Source: Chouinard (2015)

Apply that sequence to the SNAP example

Setting and agents SNAP-authorized retailers respond to a revised stocking standard

Choice Each retailer decides whether authorization remains worthwhile

Constraint Continued authorization may require costly changes in qualifying inventory

Result Some retailers may lose authorization when compliance cost exceeds the value of participation

Empirical question How did the number type and location of authorized retailers change

Keep definitions assumptions and results distinct

Element SNAP example
Definition ai(r)=1a_i(r)=1 means retailer ii is authorized under standard rr
Maintained assumption The stronger standard raises compliance cost for at least some retailers
Model result A retailer may lose authorization when compliance cost exceeds its value

Definitions assign meaning. Assumptions establish the conditions for the reasoning. Results must follow from both.

Use notation only when it reduces the reader’s burden

For retailer ii

  • rr is the strictness of the stocking standard
  • BiB_i is the value of SNAP authorization
  • Ki(r)K_i(r) is the cost of meeting standard rr
  • ai(r)a_i(r) is authorization status

The index ii matters because retailer values and costs differ. Additional notation should be introduced only when it changes or clarifies the argument.

Chouinard recommends recognizable notation defined without delay and used consistently. Ask whether each symbol earns its place by reducing repetition or clarifying heterogeneity.

Make the equation readable

The retailer remains authorized when the value of authorization covers the compliance cost

ai(r)={1if Bi≥Ki(r)0if Bi<Ki(r) a_i(r)= \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } B_i\geq K_i(r)\\ 0 & \text{if } B_i<K_i(r) \end{cases}

In words a higher-cost standard can change authorization only for retailers near enough to the participation threshold.

Define the symbols before or immediately after displaying the equation. Then give the economic interpretation. The equation and prose should make the same claim.

A model result is not yet an empirical finding

Model result

Holding BiB_i fixed a higher Ki(r)K_i(r) can cause a retailer to lose authorization

Empirical hypothesis

Retailers expected to face higher compliance costs are more likely to lose authorization after implementation

Evidence

Store-year authorization data can show whether losses differ by observable retailer characteristics

The evidence may support or challenge the hypothesis without revealing the unobserved compliance cost itself.

Discussion: apply Chouinard to your research

  1. What role does theory serve in your paper
  2. Are you applying modifying or developing a model
  3. Which concept must be defined before the reader sees an equation
  4. Which assumption carries your principal result
  5. What empirical hypothesis follows from that result

Use whole-class discussion. Students may answer using a dissertation chapter or another current project. Ask for the relationship among the five answers rather than separate summaries.

Cheng

A defensible argument has a beginning middle and end

Cheng describes a proof as a sequence of statements in which each statement follows from what came before (Cheng 2004).

Part Function in an economic argument
Beginning Definitions assumptions and accepted results
Middle The steps connecting the premises to the result
End The claim the argument was constructed to establish

The reader should not have to supply a missing step.

Plan backward and write forward

When developing an argument it is useful to ask

What would have to be true for this conclusion to follow?

That question works backward from the intended result and helps identify the required assumptions and intermediate steps.

The written argument should then proceed forward from the stated premises to the result.

Cheng distinguishes the order in which a proof may be discovered from the order in which it should be written. The same distinction applies to an economic argument.

[Sources] - Cheng (2004/Sources)

Apply the beginning middle and end

Beginning

The revised standard raises Ki(r)K_i(r) for some retailers while BiB_i is held fixed

Middle

A retailer remains authorized only when Bi≥Ki(r)B_i\geq K_i(r)

End

A retailer that crosses the participation threshold loses authorization

Each part is necessary but each part has a different role.

Show the threshold crossing

Suppose the standard changes from r0r_0 to r1r_1 and

Ki(r0)≤Bi<Ki(r1) K_i(r_0)\leq B_i<K_i(r_1)

Then

ai(r0)=1andai(r1)=0 a_i(r_0)=1 \quad\text{and}\quad a_i(r_1)=0

The retailer was authorized under the earlier standard and is not authorized under the stronger standard.

This is a conditional model result. It does not establish that the cost increase occurred or caused an observed authorization loss in the data.

Do not take a flying leap

The authorization model can support

higher compliance cost→possible authorization loss \text{higher compliance cost} \rightarrow \text{possible authorization loss}

It does not by itself support

authorization loss→diet or health change \text{authorization loss} \rightarrow \text{diet or health change}

The second claim requires additional relationships and evidence about inventory availability purchases and health.

Cheng calls an unjustified move between statements a flying leap. The problem is not that the conclusion is impossible. The problem is that the stated premises do not establish it.

[Sources] - Cheng (2004/Sources)

Two other errors to avoid

Incorrect converse

A retailer remained authorized therefore the revised requirement was slack

The retailer may instead have changed its inventory to comply.

Unstated causal assumption

A retailer lost authorization after implementation therefore the revised rule caused the loss

The analysis must distinguish policy exposure from closure reclassification and other changes over time.

Audit these claims

A. A higher compliance cost can reduce authorization when the value of authorization is held fixed

B. A retailer that remains authorized must have faced a slack requirement

C. Authorization losses concentrated in high-SNAP communities show that the rule reduced diet quality

For each claim identify

  • What follows from the model
  • Which assumption is required
  • What additional evidence would be needed

What follows from the argument?

Claim A follows conditionally from the participation model

Claim B does not follow because continued authorization is consistent with either prior compliance or an inventory adjustment

Claim C may identify a targeting concern for retailer access but it does not establish a diet-quality effect

Reveal each response after discussion. For Claim C separate the distribution of retailer losses from later outcomes in the policy pathway.

Discussion: apply Cheng to your research

  1. What is the final claim your framework is intended to establish
  2. Which premises must be stated before that claim
  3. Which intermediate step is most likely to be omitted
  4. Have you treated a sufficient condition as if it were necessary
  5. Where might your prose claim more than the model establishes

Students should use their own research. Ask for one explicit inference in the form “Given A and B, C follows because…” and examine whether another premise is missing.

Organize the written conceptual framework

  1. State the research problem that requires explanation
  2. Identify the relevant economic theory
  3. Define agents choices constraints and key terms
  4. State the maintained assumptions
  5. Derive and interpret the model result
  6. Translate the result into an empirical hypothesis
  7. State what the model and available evidence do not establish

The order should help the reader reconstruct the argument without reverse engineering it.

Framework measurement and identification answer different questions

Conceptual framework

Why might the stocking standard change retailer authorization

Measurement

Which observed variables represent authorization retailer type location and program reliance

Identification

What comparison isolates the response to the policy from other changes over time

The conceptual framework informs the design but does not substitute for it.

First draft: where does the argument overreach?

The revised rule increased required inventory and therefore improved food access. Stores unable to meet the rule lost authorization, so we expect fewer authorized retailers and healthier inventories after 2018.

Ask whether the paragraph

  • States the maintained assumptions
  • Separates model results from empirical hypotheses
  • Distinguishes observed from unobserved outcomes
  • Limits the conclusion to what the evidence can support

Revised paragraph

The revised standard may raise the cost of continued authorization for retailers that did not already satisfy it. Holding the value of authorization fixed, a retailer loses authorization when the compliance cost exceeds that value. We therefore test whether authorization losses increased after implementation and whether losses varied by store type and local SNAP participation. Because the data do not measure inventory, purchases, or health, these outcomes remain outside the empirical claim.

The revision states the mechanism prediction empirical application and boundary in sequence.

Boundaries strengthen the argument

The framework supports a conditional prediction about retailer authorization.

The data can describe changes in the number type and location of authorized retailers.

The study cannot directly establish

  • Which retailers faced a binding inventory constraint
  • How store inventory changed
  • How household purchases changed
  • Whether diet or health improved

Apply the reporting sequence to your research

Draft four connected statements

  1. Mechanism Theory implies that ___ affects ___ because ___
  2. Condition This prediction requires ___
  3. Evidence I observe the relevant concepts using ___
  4. Boundary These data do not establish ___

Then check whether each statement follows from the preceding one.

A reader should be able to answer six questions

  1. What problem does the framework explain
  2. Which theory organizes the explanation
  3. What do the central terms and symbols mean
  4. Which assumptions produce the result
  5. What empirical hypothesis follows
  6. What remains outside the model or evidence

If one answer is missing the written framework is incomplete.

What the readings contribute

Chouinard identifies the components and order of a theory section

Cheng requires each conclusion to follow from stated premises

Together they turn a model into a written argument whose scope a reader can evaluate.
