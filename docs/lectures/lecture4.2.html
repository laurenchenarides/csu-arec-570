Review the following claim

Because stricter stocking standards require healthier foods, they improve food access and diet quality.

Use the retailer model to assess the claim

What does the model add?

The standard may raise compliance costs and cause some retailers to lose authorization which can reduce the number of SNAP outlets in a community

What do the available data observe?

The quoted claim describes an intended policy pathway. The retailer participation model adds another pathway through compliance cost and authorization. Ask students to distinguish the model result from the later outcomes that would require separate measures and relationships.

Chouinard: Structuring theory

Theory in an empirical paper

Chouinard emphasizes making the theory that motivates the research question and hypotheses explicit (Chouinard 2015).

For an empirical paper the theory section can

  • Explain the mechanism behind the research question
  • State the assumptions needed for that mechanism
  • Derive the hypotheses evaluated with data
  • Connect theoretical concepts to empirical measures
  • State the limits of the conclusions supported by the evidence

The role of theory depends on the contribution

Theoretical paper Empirical paper
Develop or modify a model Apply theory to explain the empirical question
Establish analytical results Derive hypotheses and comparative predictions
Show what changes relative to prior theory Connect concepts to observed measures
Treat the model as a central contribution Use the model to organize the empirical argument

Both require explicit assumptions and a coherent sequence of reasoning.

Match the detail to the theoretical contribution

Apply established theory

Summarize the relevant result and explain how it applies to the research problem

Modify established theory

State the baseline model, identify the change, and show how the result changes

Develop new theory

Define the setting, assumptions, relationships, and results in sufficient detail to establish the contribution

The appropriate level of detail depends on how theory contributes to the paper.

Chouinard cautions against rederiving familiar theory when the contribution is an application. More detail is warranted when the paper changes the model or contributes new theory.

Present the model in a logical order

Chouinard recommends introducing the model components before presenting the complete argument.

  1. Define the setting and relevant agents
  2. Identify choices and institutional constraints
  3. State the maintained assumptions
  4. Introduce the relationships among model objects
  5. Derive and interpret the conditional result
  6. Connect the result to the empirical hypothesis

Apply the sequence: setting and constraint

Setting and agents SNAP-authorized retailers respond to a revised stocking standard

Choice Each retailer decides whether authorization remains worthwhile

Institutional constraint A retailer must satisfy the stocking standard to remain eligible for SNAP authorization

The stocking rule limits the retailer’s feasible choices. A compliance cost is a consequence of responding to that rule rather than the constraint itself.

Use constraint for a limit on choice imposed by the setting. Here the eligibility rule is the constraint. Do not use constraint as a synonym for an assumption or a cost.

Apply the sequence: assumptions relationship and result

Maintained assumptions

These are conditions accepted for the model comparison

  • The stronger standard does not reduce compliance cost and may raise it by different amounts across retailers
  • BiB_i is held fixed when comparing the two standards
  • Retailers compare the benefit of authorization with the cost of compliance

Relationship ai(r)=1a_i(r)=1 when Bi≥Ki(r)B_i\geq K_i(r)

Conditional result Authorization changes when Ki(r)K_i(r) rises from no greater than BiB_i to greater than BiB_i

Interpret the central terms consistently

For retailer ii

  • rr is the strictness of the stocking standard
  • BiB_i is the benefit of authorization including SNAP-related sales
  • Ki(r)K_i(r) is the cost of meeting standard rr
  • ai(r)a_i(r) is authorization status

The index ii allows benefits and costs to differ across retailers.

The benefit of authorization is not a direct government payout. It represents the private benefit to the retailer including SNAP-related revenue and access to customers who use SNAP benefits. Chouinard recommends recognizable notation defined without delay and used consistently.

Pair the equation with its interpretation

The retailer remains authorized when the benefit of authorization covers the compliance cost

ai(r)={1if Bi≥Ki(r)0if Bi<Ki(r) a_i(r)= \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } B_i\geq K_i(r)\\ 0 & \text{if } B_i<K_i(r) \end{cases}

A change in the standard changes authorization only when compliance cost moves from at or below BiB_i to above BiB_i.

Define the symbols before or immediately after displaying the equation. Then give the economic interpretation. The equation and prose should make the same claim.

Separate the model result from the empirical hypothesis

Model result

Holding BiB_i fixed, a higher Ki(r)K_i(r) can cause a retailer to lose authorization

Empirical hypothesis

Retailers expected to face higher compliance costs are more likely to lose authorization after implementation

Evidence

Store-year authorization data can show whether losses differ by observable retailer characteristics

The evidence may support or challenge the hypothesis without revealing the unobserved compliance cost itself.

  4. Which assumption carries your principal result
  5. What empirical hypothesis follows from that result

Cheng: Explicit reasoning

Cheng’s structure: premises reasoning and conclusion

Cheng describes a proof as a sequence of statements in which each statement follows from what came before (Cheng 2004).

Part Function in an economic argument
Beginning Definitions assumptions and accepted results
Middle The steps connecting the premises to the result
End The claim the argument was constructed to establish

The written argument makes the intermediate steps visible.

Develop the argument backward and present it forward

When developing an argument it is useful to ask

What would have to be true for this conclusion to follow?

That question works backward from the intended result and helps identify the required assumptions and intermediate steps.

The written argument should then proceed forward from the stated premises to the result.

Cheng distinguishes the order in which a proof may be discovered from the order in which it should be written. The same distinction applies to an economic argument.

[Sources] - Cheng (2004/Sources)

Apply Cheng’s structure to the retailer model

Beginning

The revised standard may raise Ki(r)K_i(r) while BiB_i is held fixed

Middle

A retailer remains authorized only when Bi≥Ki(r)B_i\geq K_i(r)

End

For a retailer whose compliance cost rises above the benefit of authorization the model predicts a change from authorized to unauthorized

Show the threshold crossing

Suppose the standard changes from r0r_0 to r1r_1 and

Ki(r0)≤Bi<Ki(r1) K_i(r_0)\leq B_i<K_i(r_1)

Then

ai(r0)=1andai(r1)=0 a_i(r_0)=1 \quad\text{and}\quad a_i(r_1)=0

The retailer was authorized under the earlier standard and is not authorized under the stronger standard.

This is a conditional model result. It does not establish that the cost increase occurred or caused an observed authorization loss in the data.

Cheng’s handwaving problem

Cheng uses handwaving for a step that may be plausible but is not established by the stated argument

The authorization model can support

higher compliance cost→possible authorization loss \text{higher compliance cost} \rightarrow \text{possible authorization loss}

It does not by itself establish

authorization loss→food access change→diet or health change \text{authorization loss} \rightarrow \text{food access change} \rightarrow \text{diet or health change}

That downstream pathway requires relationships and evidence about inventory availability purchases and health.

Handwaving does not mean the conclusion is impossible. It means that the premises and intermediate steps presented so far do not establish it. In this example the omitted steps include whether households used an affected retailer whether alternative retailers replace that access and whether purchases respond.

[Sources] - Cheng (2004/Sources)

Two claims that require more support

Invalid converse

A retailer remained authorized therefore the revised requirement was slack

The retailer may instead have changed its inventory to comply.

Unsupported causal attribution

A retailer lost authorization after implementation therefore the revised rule caused the loss

The analysis needs to distinguish policy exposure from closure, reclassification, and other changes over time.

Organizing a written conceptual framework

  1. State the research problem that requires explanation
  2. Identify the relevant economic theory
  3. Define agents choices institutional constraints and key terms
  4. State the maintained assumptions
  5. Introduce the relationships among model objects
  6. Derive the result and empirical hypothesis
  7. State what the model and available evidence do not establish

The sequence should make the relationship between theory, prediction, and evidence clear.

From the framework to empirical design

Step Question SNAP application
Framework What mechanism produces the prediction A stronger standard may change authorization through compliance cost
Measurement Which variables represent the concepts Authorization retailer type location and local SNAP participation
Identification What comparison isolates the policy response A comparison to what authorization would have been without the change

The data do not directly measure BiB_i, Ki(r)K_i(r), or store inventory

Identification is the argument that the empirical comparison separates the policy response from other changes over time. This slide introduces the concept. Identification strategies and threats receive fuller treatment in Unit 6.

Consider the following paragraph

The revised rule increased required inventory and therefore improved food access. Stores unable to meet the rule lost authorization, so we expect fewer authorized retailers and healthier inventories after 2018.

Consider whether the paragraph

  • States the maintained assumptions
  • Separates model results from empirical hypotheses
  • Distinguishes observed from unobserved outcomes
  • Limits the conclusion to what the evidence can support

Revised paragraph

The revised standard may raise the cost of continued SNAP authorization with the increase varying across retailers. Let BiB_i denote the benefit a retailer receives from authorization including SNAP-related sales and let Ki(r)K_i(r) denote the compliance cost. Holding BiB_i fixed the model predicts an authorization loss when Ki(r)K_i(r) exceeds BiB_i. This prediction motivates an analysis of authorization changes by store type and local SNAP participation. The available data do not establish changes in inventory household access purchases diet or health.

The revision states the mechanism, prediction, empirical application, and boundary in sequence.

State the scope of the evidence

The framework supports a conditional prediction about retailer authorization.

The data can describe changes in the number, type, and location of authorized retailers.

The study cannot directly establish

  • Which retailers changed inventory and by how much
  • How household access to qualifying food changed
  • How household purchases changed
  • Whether diet or health improved

Apply the reporting sequence to your research

Use one current research question to consider

  1. What mechanism connects the cause or policy to the outcome
  2. Which maintained assumptions are required for the prediction
  3. Which theoretical concepts have observable counterparts
  4. What empirical comparison would isolate the proposed effect
  5. Which conclusions remain outside the model or available evidence

Summary of readings

Chouinard identifies the components and order of a theory section

Cheng requires each conclusion to follow from stated premises

Both provide guidelines to turn a model into a written argument whose scope a reader can evaluate.
