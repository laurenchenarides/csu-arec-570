Example: SNAP retailer stocking requirements

SNAP-authorized retailers must satisfy program eligibility standards. Eligibility requires a minimum assortment of staple foods.

In 2018, a new rule went into effect that changed the minimum number of items per food category to remain eligible.

Changes in how qualifying varieties and stocking units are defined may affect

  • Products carried by participating retailers
  • Cost of remaining SNAP authorized
  • Retailer entry into the program and/or continued participation and/or exit
  • Access to staple foods for SNAP households

Keep the policy description brief. The purpose is to establish the research problem rather than provide the full institutional background.

The core policy issue

Policy concern

Previous minimum standards could allow SNAP participation with limited nutritious variety

Policy intervention

Require more varieties across staple food categories and more perishable foods

Intended mechanism

Use the value of SNAP authorization to encourage neighborhood retailers to stock more nutritious foods

Longer-run goal

Improve convenient access to foods consistent with dietary guidance in low-income and rural communities

This is the policy rationale. The empirical project evaluates whether the intended mechanism occurs and whether retailer responses alter the result.

The rulemaking record is available through Federal Register docket FNS-2025-0018.

Translating the policy issue into a research problem

Intended stocking pathway

stocking requirement→retailer inventory→local availability→purchases and health \text{stocking requirement} \rightarrow \text{retailer inventory} \rightarrow \text{local availability} \rightarrow \text{purchases and health}

Retailer participation pathway

stocking requirement→compliance cost→SNAP authorization→authorized-store portfolio \text{stocking requirement} \rightarrow \text{compliance cost} \rightarrow \text{SNAP authorization} \rightarrow \text{authorized-store portfolio}

For purposes of this example, the application observes the second pathway.

Inventory remains a theoretical mechanism rather than an observed outcome.

Begin with a researchable problem

Broad policy question

Do stronger stocking standards improve access to staple foods?

Researchable questions

  1. How do revised standards affect continued SNAP authorization
  2. Which types of retailers are more likely to lose authorization
  3. How does the portfolio and location of authorized retailers change
  4. Are authorization losses concentrated in areas with a higher share of SNAP-participating households?

If losses are concentrated where SNAP participation is highest then the policy change may be poorly targeted with respect to retailer access.

This question connects the distribution of retailer losses to the communities the program is intended to serve.

Where does the conceptual framework enter?

There is no universal order but each part has a distinct role.

  • The problem statement identifies the possible loss of SNAP-authorized retailers
  • The literature review supplies theory and evidence about regulation retailer participation and food access
  • The conceptual framework explains why the rule may change authorization and predicts which stores and communities are most affected
  • The methods use annual store authorization and location data to evaluate those predictions

The framework states the expected relationships. The empirical design specifies how the evidence will be used to estimate them.

Ethridge, Chapter 8

Ethridge: conceptualize the problem

For Ethridge, a conceptual framework is an analysis of a specific research problem using relevant theory and concepts (Ethridge 2004).

The framework applies theory to explain the relationships that create or characterize the problem.

It is not a review of standard theory and it is not a description of the empirical method.

Five elements of a conceptual framework

Ethridge draws on Williams to describe an organized analysis that may include

  • Sources of the problem
  • Alternative solutions to the problem
  • Variables relevant to the analysis
  • Relationships organized into a system
  • Hypotheses about expected results

Source: Ethridge Chapter 8 pages 130–131

This five-item list comes directly from Ethridge’s discussion of Williams. It replaces the earlier six-item checklist which was a synthesis rather than a list stated by Ethridge.

How Ethridge recommends proceeding

  1. Review how related problems have been conceptualized
  2. Reduce the problem to its simplest useful form
  3. Identify the applicable economic theory
  4. Construct a base model using maintained hypotheses
  5. Add dimensions only when they improve the analysis
  6. Assemble hypotheses from the conceptual reasoning

Source: Ethridge Chapter 8 pages 136–138

Applying the five elements to the SNAP example

Source of the problem Some SNAP retailers may offer limited nutritious variety

Proposed solution Stronger stocking standards

Relevant variables Authorization status, store type, location, and neighborhood-level SNAP participation rates

Relationship Compliance costs alter the retailer’s decision to remain authorized

Expected result Authorization losses vary across store types and communities

Three kinds of hypotheses in Ethridge

Type Role in the conceptual framework
Maintained hypotheses Assumptions accepted for purposes of the analysis
Diagnostic hypotheses Proposed explanations for the source or nature of the problem
Remedial hypotheses Proposed actions expected to address the diagnosed problem

Maintained hypotheses define the conditions for the reasoning. Diagnostic hypotheses explain the problem. Remedial hypotheses connect a proposed intervention to a response.

Apply the three hypothesis types

Maintained hypothesis

Meeting the stronger stocking standard is costly for at least some retailers

Diagnostic hypothesis

Some SNAP households have limited access to nutritious foods because nearby retailers stock too few qualifying products

Remedial hypothesis

A stronger stocking standard increases nutritious inventory among retailers that comply and remain authorized

Stress that maintained hypotheses are analytically distinct from diagnostic and remedial hypotheses. The example statements are starting points that require refinement as the model is developed.

Inventory is the policy mechanism in this example but it is not observed in the proposed empirical application. The observed response is whether a store remains authorized.

The framework also identifies competing responses

The intended policy mechanism is not the only possible response.

  • Some retailers already meet the new standard
  • Some retailers change inventory and remain authorized
  • Some retailers decide that compliance is not worthwhile
  • Some retailers lose authorization
  • Authorization losses may be concentrated by store type or community

The framework distinguishes the unobserved stocking mechanism from the observed authorization outcome.

Discussion: apply Ethridge to your research

  1. What specific condition or relationship requires explanation in your project
  2. Which theory helps you reason through that problem
  3. What maintained hypothesis does your reasoning require
  4. What diagnostic hypothesis follows from the framework
  5. What remedial hypothesis would correspond to a proposed intervention

Students can answer using their own dissertation idea or another current project. The questions do not require knowledge of SNAP policy.

Varian

From the concept to the model

Ethridge asks what the conceptual framework must accomplish

Apply relevant theory to the research problem and derive meaningful hypotheses

Varian asks how the economic model should be structured

Identify agents choices objectives constraints interactions and adjustment

Varian: start with questions

  1. Who makes choices
  2. What can each agent choose
  3. What objective guides each choice
  4. What constraints limit each agent
  5. How do the agents interact
  6. What adjusts when choices are not mutually consistent

Work through the simplest example that retains the mechanism. Add complexity only when it changes the result (Varian 1997).

Structure the example: agents and choices

Who makes choices and what can they choose?

Retailers decide whether the value of SNAP authorization justifies the cost of meeting the rule

Households choose among the retailers available to them

The regulator sets stocking standards and determines eligibility

Pause after the question and solicit possibilities before revealing each response.

Structure the example: objectives and constraints

What do agents want and what limits their choices?

Retailers value SNAP-related revenue and face compliance procurement shelf-space and spoilage costs

Households value food and other goods and face income travel time prices and product availability

The regulator seeks healthier food access and program integrity within administrative constraints

Pause after the question and let students identify objectives and constraints before revealing the example.

Structure the example: interaction and adjustment

How do choices interact and what adjusts?

The stocking standard changes compliance costs for some retailers

The value of SNAP customers affects a retailer’s willingness to incur those costs

Retailers enter remain in or leave the authorized-store network

The number type and location of authorized stores adjust across communities

When does the rule force a change?

Requires a change — binding

The new standard requires a retailer to change its inventory to remain eligible

Already satisfied — slack

The retailer already meets the standard so the rule does not require an inventory change

Inventory without the program minimum — unconstrained inventory

The inventory a retailer would choose without the program minimum. It does not mean that the retailer is exempt from the rule.

The earlier version referred to standard concavity conditions. In optimization this means the objective has a well-behaved single optimum such as profits with diminishing marginal returns. That assumption is not needed for the simplified authorization model and has been removed.

Without inventory data the empirical analysis cannot identify which individual stores faced a binding standard. The distinction remains useful as a theoretical explanation for why the policy affects some stores more than others.

Developing a conceptual model

For market or neighborhood mm the number of authorized retailers is

Am(r)=∑i∈mai(r) A_m(r)=\sum_{i\in m}a_i(r)

The portfolio also describes

  • The number of authorized stores
  • The types of stores that remain
  • Their geographic distribution
  • The communities that experience losses

Here Am(r)A_m(r) is the number of authorized retailers in market mm under stocking standard rr. The outcome is the authorized-store network rather than the quantity of inventory.

Program targeting

Let SmS_m denote the share of households participating in SNAP in community mm.

If authorization losses are larger where SmS_m is higher then the authorized-store network contracts most in communities that rely more heavily on SNAP.

That pattern would suggest a targeting problem relative to the policy’s food-access goal.

It would not by itself establish the effect on store inventory household purchases diet or health.

Maintained hypotheses behind the result

  • Meeting the new standard is more costly for some retailers than for others
  • SNAP authorization has positive but heterogeneous value across retailers
  • Retailers can remain authorized lose authorization or operate outside SNAP
  • The policy can change the authorized-store portfolio even when inventory is not observed

These maintained hypotheses support the authorization mechanism. They do not establish its empirical magnitude.

Match the model to available evidence

Mechanisms outside the available data

  • Inventory changes explain how stores may comply
  • Compliance costs explain why authorization responses may differ
  • SNAP revenue explains why authorization is more valuable to some stores
  • Household purchases diet and health lie farther along the policy pathway

These mechanisms motivate the framework but are not outcomes that this application can test directly.

Romer

From the model to the argument

Varian provides the structure of the economic model

Agents choices objectives constraints interactions and adjustment

Romer asks whether the model supports the claims made for it

Words symbols and empirical interpretations must remain connected

Romer: keep words and symbols connected

Romer’s concern is not the use of mathematics. It is slippage among

  • What the verbal argument says
  • What the mathematical model establishes
  • What the empirical evidence can support

Mathematical notation improves the argument only when terms are defined consistently and the conclusion follows from the stated assumptions (Romer 2015).

Audit the claim

Claim

Stricter stocking requirements improve food access

Claim supported by the available outcomes

Revised stocking standards may change the number type and location of SNAP-authorized retailers

If authorization losses are concentrated in high-SNAP communities then the pattern is inconsistent with the policy’s targeting objective.

What the available data do not establish

The available data do not establish

  • How store inventory changed
  • Whether remaining stores offer more nutritious foods
  • How household purchases changed
  • Whether diet or health improved

What belongs in the written framework?

For your project the conceptual framework should state

  1. The problem that requires explanation
  2. The relevant theory and concepts
  3. The agents choices objectives and constraints
  4. The mechanisms connecting the proposed cause to the outcome
  5. The maintained diagnostic and remedial hypotheses
  6. The conditions under which each prediction applies

Research-design implications

The framework generates design questions

  • What is the appropriate unit of analysis
  • How will loss of authorization be distinguished from store closure
  • Which store characteristics define the portfolio
  • Which geography defines the relevant community
  • How will local SNAP participation be measured
  • When can each outcome respond
  • Which adjustments could affect the comparison group

These questions should inform the empirical design.

Apply the sequence to your research

  1. Problem State the condition that requires explanation
  2. Theory Identify the concepts that help explain it
  3. Structure Identify agents choices objectives constraints and adjustment
  4. Prediction State what should change and under which conditions
  5. Boundary State what the model does not establish
  6. Evidence Identify an observation that would challenge the proposed mechanism
